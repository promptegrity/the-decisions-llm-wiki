# Decision jobs

Entry point for this wiki. Pick the **job** that matches your situation, then open a model.

This catalog is organized by *what you are trying to do*.

## What is a model?

A model is a compact way of seeing a situation so you can think and act more clearly. In this wiki, each page under [models](models/index.md) is one such lens.

1. **Models help simplify**  
   They reduce a messy reality to a few variables you can work with — without pretending the world is that simple.

2. **They recapitulate**  
   They pack experience, research, or hard-won practice into a short structure you can reuse.

3. **They are visual**  
   Most are diagrams, matrices, or spatial metaphors — so you can *see* options, trade-offs, and positions at a glance.

4. **They are methods**  
   They are not only descriptions; they are procedures for asking better questions and choosing a next step.

## Why do we need models?

Without a model, every decision starts from scratch and stays tangled in detail. Models give shared language, speed, and a place to stand — so you can compare options, notice blind spots, and decide (or understand) with intention. They are scaffolding, not truth: use them, then leave them when the situation demands more nuance.

## Navigate

1. Match the situation to a **job** (row above) — or start from a [situation path](models/paths/index.md) / [routing table](models/routing.md).
2. Open the family hub under [models](models/index.md).
3. Choose a model whose one-line intent matches the question.
4. **Apply it with the user** — walk `### Facilitation steps` (or `# How it works`) one step per turn; do not skip to a naked recommendation.
5. Follow `# Relations` (or the next path step) for linked models.

Agents using this wiki are **read-only**: they facilitate with you; they do not edit the catalog.
