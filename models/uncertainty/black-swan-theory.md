# Overview

**Black Swan** theory (Nassim Nicholas Taleb) names events that are **rare**, **extreme in impact**, and **predictable only in hindsight** — after which we invent a tidy story. The warning: past experience and smooth statistics can make you *feel* smarter while leaving you fragile to the event that matters most.

understanding others’ (and systems’) vulnerability to the unimagined.

# When to use

- Plans assume the future is a mild variant of the past.
- Risk models rely on thin historical samples or Gaussian comfort.
- Success narratives claim foresight that was luck or survivorship.
- You need humility about prediction in domains with fat tails (markets, tech, geopolitics, pandemics).

Skip it when you can inventory concrete knowledge gaps (use [Rumsfeld](/models/uncertainty/rumsfeld-matrix.md)) or map layered defenses for known hazards (use [Swiss Cheese](/models/uncertainty/swiss-cheese-model.md)).

# How it works

### Three attributes (Taleb)

1. **Outlier** — outside regular expectations; nothing in the past convincingly pointed to it.
2. **Extreme impact** — changes the game, not a small variance.
3. **Retrospective narrativity** — after the fact, we explain it as if it were foreseeable.

### Epistemic lesson

| Habit | Problem |
|-------|---------|
| Extrapolating from experience | Experience samples the quiet middle, not the tail |
| Storytelling after shocks | Confuses explanation with prediction |
| Optimizing for average cases | Can increase exposure to ruin |

### Questions

1. What would a **ruinous** or **transformative** surprise look like here — even if we can’t assign a probability?
2. Are we mistaking **absence of evidence** for **evidence of absence**?
3. Does our strategy gain from **optionality** (upside open, downside capped) or from fragile precision?
4. Whose **narrative** makes this look under control?

### How to act

1. **Prefer robustness / antifragility** over precise forecasts in fat-tailed domains.
2. **Stress the tails** — scenarios, buffers, redundancy — not only expected values.
3. **Distrust neat postmortems** that claim “we should have known.”
4. **Keep skin in the game** — incentives that punish false confidence.

### Practice tip

In strategy reviews, add one agenda item: “What Black Swan would make this plan look foolish — and what optionality do we have if it arrives?” If the answer is only “unlikely,” you have not engaged the model.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Event** — What rare shock or narrative of the past are we discussing?
2. **Attributes** — Outlier? Extreme impact? Retrospective “obvious”?
3. **Induction trap** — Where were we overconfident from past calm?
4. **Exposure** — What fragile bets depend on “normal” continuing?
5. **Robustness** — One change that reduces ruin risk without predicting the next swan?
