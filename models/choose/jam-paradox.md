# Overview

The Jam Paradox (choice overload) says that **more options often reduce** decisions and satisfaction: people browse longer, buy less, and regret more. Limiting the set is not deprivation — it is how you keep agency. Named after research that compared a large jam assortment with a small one: the small set drove more purchases.

For personal decisions: prune first, then choose. Endless menus feel like freedom and behave like paralysis.

# When to use

- You keep researching tools, courses, jobs, or features without picking.
- A longlist of “good” options leaves you anxious or numb.
- You reverse decisions often because something else might have been better.
- Your portfolio or backlog has grown into a fake marketplace of selves.

Skip it when you only have two poles and the conflict is values (use [Rubber Band Model](/models/choose/rubber-band-model.md)), when you need depth on one option (use [SWOT Analysis](/models/prioritize/swot-analysis.md)), or when the goal itself is still foggy (use [SMART Model](/models/prioritize/smart-model.md)).

# How it works

### The pattern

1. **Too many options** → comparison cost rises, criteria blur, fear of missing out spikes.
2. **Decision quality drops** — you delay, defer to defaults, or pick then second-guess.
3. **Satisfaction falls** — even a good choice feels worse because the rejected set stays vivid.

### How to limit choices on purpose

1. **Define a filter before browsing** — e.g. budget, time, “must support X,” non-negotiables.
2. **Cap the longlist** — hard max (often 3–5 contenders); park the rest out of sight.
3. **Use a default** — “if nothing beats current within a week, keep current.”
4. **Decide with a rule** — once the shortlist exists, apply [Yes/No Rule](/models/choose/yes-no-rule.md) or a simple score, then stop researching.
5. **Hide the graveyard** — archived options should not reappear in daily view.

### Simple table

| Stage | Action |
|-------|--------|
| Open field | Apply 2–3 must-have filters |
| Longlist | Cut to ≤5 |
| Shortlist | Compare only on the same criteria |
| Choice | Commit; schedule a review date if needed — do not keep shopping |

### Practice tip

Treat “I might need more options” as a craving, not a requirement. Add options only when the shortlist fails a written criterion — not when anxiety asks for another tab.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Pile** — How many options are you considering, and what decision is stalled?
2. **Cost** — How is the long list hurting you (delay, regret, shallow compare)?
3. **Cut** — Reduce to a small set (e.g. 3–5) with an explicit filter.
4. **Compare** — Compare only the shortlist; park the rest without reopening yet.
5. **Pick** — Choose from the shortlist (or use another model on those few).
