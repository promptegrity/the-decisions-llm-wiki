# Overview

The Consequences Model argues for **deciding early**: every delay is itself a choice, and it usually *narrows* what remains possible. Waiting feels like keeping options open; in practice, other people move, calendars fill, and reversible paths become costly.

The model trains you to look ahead along chains of consequences — not to predict the future perfectly, but to see that late decisions often leave only bad leftovers.

### Diagram

```text
  time →
  now: many options open
  later: options shrink as commitments land
  decide early on reversible moves when delay is costly
```

# When to use

- You are postponing a choice “until you know more,” yet the situation is already evolving.
- Paths fork soon (accept an offer, book travel, hire, launch, commit publicly).
- Indecision is creating default outcomes you would never pick on purpose.
- You need to explain to others *why* a rough decision now beats a perfect one later.

Skip it when information genuinely arrives on a fixed date and waiting is cheap — or when revising later is the skill you need (use [Stop Rule](/models/choose/stop-rule.md)). For pure speed without foresight, use [Yes/No Rule](/models/choose/yes-no-rule.md).

# How it works

### 1. Name the decision and the clock

Write: *What must be true by when?* If there is no date, invent a review date — open-ended delay is how options die quietly.

### 2. Sketch consequence chains

For each live option (including “do nothing”), list 2–3 steps ahead:

- **If I choose A now** → likely next constraints and freedoms
- **If I wait** → what closes, who else decides, what becomes expensive

“Do nothing” belongs on the page as an active branch, not as neutrality.

### 3. Prefer early reversible moves

When uncertainty is high, favor decisions that:

1. **Preserve learning** — you find out something important soon.
2. **Stay reversible** — cheap to undo relative to the cost of waiting.
3. **Block worse defaults** — stop the calendar from choosing for you.

### Questions to force timing honesty

1. What becomes *impossible* if I wait two more weeks?
2. Whose decision am I outsourcing by delaying?
3. Am I waiting for data — or for emotional comfort?
4. What is the smallest decision I can make today that keeps better futures open?

### Practice tip

Decide the *direction* early and the *details* later. Early does not mean reckless detail; it means locking the fork before the tree of consequences collapses to a stump.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Decision + clock** — What must be decided, and what real deadline or window exists?
2. **Chains** — For 2–3 timing choices (decide now / wait / never), sketch likely consequence chains.
3. **Options shrink** — How do waiting and commitments shrink future options?
4. **Reversible** — What early reversible move preserves option value?
5. **Commit timing** — When will you decide, and what will you stop waiting for?
