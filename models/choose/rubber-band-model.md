# Overview

The Rubber Band Model treats a dilemma as two forces on a stretched band: **what attracts you** toward one option and **what holds you** to the other (or to the status quo). Instead of forcing a false “right answer,” you make the tensions visible — then decide which force to honor, reduce, or redesign.

Useful when both options are good (or both costly), and analysis alone keeps looping.

### Diagram

```text
  [ Pole A ]  <==== PULL ====  YOU  ==== HOLD ====>  [ Pole B ]
```

# When to use

- You are stuck between two attractive paths (job A vs B, stay vs leave, depth vs breadth).
- Pros/cons lists keep growing without a decision.
- The real conflict is values or identity (“who I want to become” vs “what I fear losing”), not missing data.
- You feel pulled and restrained at the same time.

Skip it when the decision is low-stakes and speed matters (use [Yes/No Rule](/models/choose/yes-no-rule.md)), when you need a structured option diagnosis (use [SWOT Analysis](/models/prioritize/swot-analysis.md)), or when choice overload is the problem (use [Jam Paradox](/models/choose/jam-paradox.md) to cut options to two).

# How it works

Imagine (or draw) a rubber band between two poles:

```text
 [ Option A / Future self ] ←—— pull —— YOU —— hold ——→ [ Option B / Status quo ]
```

### Steps

1. **Name the two poles** clearly (not “maybe something else”).
2. **List the pulls** toward A — desires, values, opportunities, energy.
3. **List the holds** toward B (or staying) — loyalty, fear, comfort, obligations, sunk cost.
4. **Rank** the strongest 2–3 forces on each side; ignore the long tail of weak reasons.
5. **Act on the tension**, not only on the lists:
 - Strengthen a pull (make A more real: trial, conversation, prototype).
 - Loosen a hold (renegotiate an obligation, address a fear with a plan).
 - Or invent a **third shape** that keeps the valuable hold without blocking the pull.

### Guiding questions

- If I remove fear/sunk cost from the “hold” list, does the answer change?
- Which force would I regret ignoring in five years?
- What small experiment would stretch the band safely before I break it?

### Practice tip

Write pulls and holds on separate sticky notes. Physically discard weak holds (“what will people think?”) when they are not true constraints. Dilemmas shrink when fake restraints leave the table.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Poles** — Name the two poles of the dilemma clearly (not “maybe something else”).
2. **Pulls** — What attracts you toward A (desires, values, opportunities)?
3. **Holds** — What holds you toward B / status quo (loyalty, fear, obligations, sunk cost)?
4. **Rank** — Which 2–3 forces on each side are strongest? Discard weak or fake restraints.
5. **Move** — Will you strengthen a pull, loosen a hold, or invent a third shape? What is the next small experiment?
