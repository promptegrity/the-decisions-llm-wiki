# Overview

The Purchase Decision Model is a structured way to **choose something complex** — a car, laptop, apartment, vendor, or course — without drowning in brochures or falling for the last demo you saw. You fix *what matters and how much* before you fall in love with a candidate.

The point is to separate criteria (your needs) from options (the market), so sales narratives do not rewrite your priorities midstream.

### Diagram

```text
  criteria + weights  →  score options  →  rank  →  sanity check  →  pick
       (before brands) 
```

# When to use

- The purchase has many attributes and a non-trivial price or lock-in.
- You keep changing your mind after each review, test drive, or sales call.
- Multiple stakeholders must agree (household, team, budget owner).
- You want a transparent rationale you can revisit later.

Skip it for trivial buys (use [Yes/No Rule](/models/choose/yes-no-rule.md) or gut after a glance), when the real problem is whether to buy at all (use [Rubber Band Model](/models/choose/rubber-band-model.md)), or when choice overload is the main issue (limit the set first — [Jam Paradox](/models/choose/jam-paradox.md)).

# How it works

### 1. List must-haves vs nice-to-haves

- **Must-haves** — deal-breakers (budget ceiling, size, safety, compatibility, deadline).
- **Nice-to-haves** — preferences you will score, not sacred cows.

Eliminate any option that fails a must-have *before* scoring charm factors.

### 2. Weight the criteria

Assign weights that sum to 100% (or 1.0). Do this **before** naming favorites.

Example (car):

| Criterion | Weight |
|-----------|--------|
| Total cost of ownership | 30% |
| Reliability / safety | 25% |
| Daily fit (space, commute) | 20% |
| Driving feel | 15% |
| Sustainability / values | 10% |

If you cannot weight criteria, you are not ready to compare cars — you are still clarifying needs.

### 3. Score options and compute

Rate each remaining option on each criterion (e.g. 1–10). Multiply by weights; sum. Treat the ranking as a decision aid, not a verdict from God.

### 4. Sanity-check with experience

- Test the top 1–2 in real conditions.
- Optionally sleep on it ([Unconscious Thought Theory](/models/choose/unconscious-thought-theory.md)) after the sheet is filled.
- Watch for weight cheating: raising “looks” after you fall for a model.

### Practice tip

Cap the candidate list (3–5). A perfect matrix over twenty options is slower than a good matrix over a shortlist — and more prone to noise.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Object** — What complex purchase or multi-criteria choice is this?
2. **Criteria** — List must-haves vs nice-to-haves before naming brands/options.
3. **Weights** — Assign relative weights to criteria (while options are still hidden if possible).
4. **Score** — Score each option against criteria; note the leader.
5. **Sanity** — Does lived experience or a deal-breaker overturn the score? Final pick?
