# Overview

The Future of Decision-Making is a **meta-model**: it does not prescribe one framework, but asks how choosing itself will change as information, algorithms, and collectives take larger roles. Decisions may shift from lone intuition toward **augmented judgment**, **prediction markets / crowds**, **automated defaults**, and **transparent trade-off dashboards** — while human accountability, values, and taste remain the scarce inputs.

Use it to prepare teams and institutions: which decisions should stay human, which should be delegated to systems, and which need new shared rules.

### Diagram

```text
  Information → Cognition/AI → Collective → Accountability
       (design who proposes, who decides, who is responsible)
```

# When to use

- You are designing governance, AI assistance, or decision rights for a product or organization.
- Stakeholders fear “the algorithm decides” or, conversely, refuse useful automation.
- You want a forward-looking conversation after cataloguing classical models.
- Strategy depends on how customers, citizens, or employees will choose tomorrow.

Skip it when you need a concrete tool for today’s meeting (pick a model from the [Decision jobs](/index.md) map instead). This page orients; it does not replace [SMART](/models/prioritize/smart-model.md) or the [Project Management Triangle](/models/prioritize/project-management-triangle.md).

# How it works

Scan four shifting layers of decision practice:

| Layer | Emerging shift | Design question |
|-------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Information** | More data, more noise | What must be filtered before a human sees it? |
| **Cognition** | Models & AI as co-pilots | Where does the machine propose vs decide? |
| **Collective** | Platforms, crowds, real-time feedback | Whose voice counts, and how is it weighted? |
| **Accountability** | Traceable logs, audits, regulation | Who is responsible when the system errs? |

### Questions for any future-facing decision system

1. **Reversibility** — Can a human override, and how fast?
2. **Values** — Which preferences are encoded as defaults (and whose)?
3. **Skill atrophy** — If we automate this choice, do people still know how to make it when the system fails?
4. **Legitimacy** — Will affected people accept the process even when they dislike the outcome?
5. **Compounding** — Which models from this wiki should remain literacy for humans who supervise machines?

### How to act

1. **Classify decisions**: automate / assist / reserve for humans — by stakes and reversibility.
2. **Keep a human-readable rationale** next to model outputs.
3. **Teach trade-off literacy** (triangles, expectations, team stages) so automation does not erase judgment.
4. **Pilot narrowly**, measure failure modes, then widen — do not flip the org to “AI decides” overnight.

### Practice tip

For one critical workflow, write: “Today a person decides X. In two years, a system will draft X; a person will confirm Y.” Make Y explicit now, or Y will silently disappear.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Domain** — Which decisions (product, org, civic) are we redesigning for the future?
2. **Layers** — Scan information, cognition/AI, collective, accountability — which is shifting fastest?
3. **Human** — What must stay human (values, taste, responsibility)?
4. **Machine** — What can propose vs decide with override?
5. **Rule** — One governance rule you will write so tomorrow’s deciding stays legible?
