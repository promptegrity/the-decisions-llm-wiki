# Overview

The Potential Trap warns that **fixed expectations** shrink experience. When you decide in advance how an event, person, or future self “should” turn out, two losses follow: disappointment when reality diverges, and blindness to better outcomes that were not on your script.

“Avoid having expectations” does not mean apathy or zero standards. It means preferring **openness and attention** over a pre-edited movie of what must happen — especially about people and unfolding situations you do not control.

### Diagram

```text
  locked SCRIPT of potential --> closes present options
  open ORIENTATION --> act without clinging to one future
```

# When to use

- You are repeatedly disappointed by holidays, launches, dates, or meetings that were “supposed to be” perfect.
- You pre-judge people and then only collect confirming evidence.
- Anticipation is more vivid than the actual experience (and the actual feels flat).
- You confuse hope/preparation with attachment to one outcome.

Skip it when you need clear performance contracts with others (use [Expectation Management Model](/models/relate/expectation-management-model.md)), or when future thinking is a structured decision tool (use [Consequences Model](/models/choose/consequences-model.md)).

# How it works

### Expectation vs orientation

| | **Expectation (trap)** | **Orientation (useful)** |
|--|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Picture | One detailed “must” outcome | Direction, values, constraints |
| Control | Assumes you can force the script | Separates influence from control |
| On surprise | Threat / failure | Information / option |
| Afterward | “It wasn’t what I imagined” | “What was it, actually?” |

### How the trap closes

1. You invent a preferred future in high resolution.
2. You invest ego in that picture.
3. Reality arrives different (as it usually does).
4. You feel robbed — and miss what *did* become possible.

### Questions

1. What exact scene am I demanding from this situation?
2. What would “going well” mean if I dropped the casting and dialogue I wrote?
3. Am I preparing (useful) or rehearsing resentment in advance (trap)?
4. Where have open outcomes previously beaten my script?

### Practice tip

Before a charged event, write the expectation in one sentence — then rewrite it as an orientation: “I will show up for X; I will notice Y; I will not need Z to go exactly like this.” Revisit after. Compare disappointment size to openness practiced.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Script** — What expectation or “potential” story are you attached to?
2. **Trap** — How does that script close options or create disappointment?
3. **Orientation** — What would open orientation look like without dropping standards?
4. **Loosen** — Which expectation can you release or rewrite today?
5. **Act open** — What next step stays flexible instead of locked to the old script?
