# Overview

Flow is the mental state where you are fully absorbed in an activity: time softens, self-consciousness drops, and the work feels effortless yet demanding. Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi argued that lasting happiness is less about passive pleasure and more about this balance — **challenge matched to skill**.

Too little challenge relative to skill → boredom. Too much challenge → anxiety. The “flow channel” sits between them. Knowing what puts *you* in that channel is a map of what makes you happy in practice, not in theory.

### Diagram

```text
  Challenge
      high |  anxiety        FLOW
           |  (too hard)
           |
      low  |  boredom / apathy
           +-----------------------> skill
              low              high
```

# When to use

- You feel busy or entertained but not fulfilled.
- You want to choose work, hobbies, or learning paths by *energy after*, not by status or habit.
- You are redesigning a role or week: what to keep, deepen, or drop.
- You notice chronic boredom (underused) or chronic stress (overstretched) in the same job.

Skip it when the question is career *timing* (use [Personal Performance Model](/models/understand-self/personal-performance-model.md)) or when you need a full life profile across domains (use [Uffe Elbæk Radar](/models/understand-self/uffe-elbaek-radar.md)).

# How it works

### The flow channel

| | **Skill low** | **Skill high** |
|--|---------------|----------------|
| **Challenge high** | Anxiety / overwhelm | **Flow** |
| **Challenge low** | Apathy / drift | Boredom / routine |

### Conditions that usually help flow

1. **Clear goals** — you know what “done well” looks like in the moment.
2. **Immediate feedback** — you can sense progress without waiting for a review cycle.
3. **Challenge ≈ skill** — stretch, not shatter; familiar, not idle.
4. **Focus without fracture** — fewer interruptions; one primary task.
5. **Intrinsic interest** — the activity is rewarding in itself, not only for the outcome.

### Questions to locate your flow activities

1. When did the last hours disappear without you watching the clock?
2. Afterward, did you feel drained in a *good* way (used) or empty (depleted)?
3. Was the difficulty slightly above your comfort — or far below / far above?
4. What would need to change (skill up, challenge down, or context) to enter the channel more often?

### Practice tip

List 5–10 recent activities and place each in the 2×2 above. Protect and expand the flow quadrant; treat chronic anxiety/boredom as design signals, not character flaws.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Activities** — List 5–10 recent activities that used real attention.
2. **Challenge vs skill** — For each: high/low challenge and high/low skill.
3. **Locate** — Which sit in anxiety, boredom, or the flow channel?
4. **Protect** — What one flow activity will you schedule or expand this week?
5. **Design** — What will you change about a non-flow activity (challenge or skill)?
