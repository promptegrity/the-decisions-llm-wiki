# Overview

The Unthinkable Model asks you to surface convictions you treat as **settled** even though you cannot prove them — faith, axioms, hunches elevated to law, cultural “of courses.” These beliefs steer decisions silently: career, loyalty, risk, love, politics.

The point is not to abolish belief. It is to label what is *evidence-based*, what is *provisional*, and what is *unthinkable to question* — so you stop mistaking dogma for data when stakes are high.

### Diagram

```text
  [proven] [weakly held] [unthinkable / untested "musts"]
                              ^
                              surface & test carefully
```

# When to use

- A debate (including with yourself) keeps looping; both sides talk past axioms.
- You feel threatened when someone questions a “basic” truth.
- Strategy or life plans rest on assumptions nobody has written down.
- You want to know which beliefs are load-bearing before a big change.

Skip it when the problem is social blind spots about *you* (use [Johari Window](/models/understand-self/johari-window.md)) or situational known/unknown risks (use [Rumsfeld Matrix](/models/uncertainty/rumsfeld-matrix.md)).

# How it works

### Three buckets of belief

1. **Provable / testable** — you can specify what would change your mind.
2. **Working hypotheses** — useful until better evidence; held lightly.
3. **Unthinkable** — identity-level; questioning feels like betrayal or chaos.

Map your strongest “musts” into these buckets. Surprise usually lives in bucket 3 dressed up as bucket 1.

### Questions to expose the unthinkable

1. What would I need to see to abandon this belief — and if the answer is “nothing,” it is unthinkable.
2. Where did I absorb this — parents, profession, subculture, one vivid failure?
3. What decisions become automatic once this belief is granted?
4. What am I afraid would fall apart if I treated it as optional?

### Practice tip

Write five sentences that start with “I know that…” Then add after each: *How do I know?* and *What would falsify it?* Move items that fail the falsification test into an explicit “axioms / faith” list you own rather than pretend are facts.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Domain** — What area of life/work are we examining (career, relationship, strategy)?
2. **Buckets** — What do you believe with proof, with weak proof, and without being able to prove?
3. **Unthinkable** — Which “must be true” has never been tested?
4. **Cost** — What would it cost if that unthinkable belief were wrong?
5. **Test** — What small test could challenge it safely?
