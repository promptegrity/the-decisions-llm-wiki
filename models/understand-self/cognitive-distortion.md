# Overview

Cognitive distortions are habitual **thinking errors** that feel like facts: mind-reading, catastrophizing, all-or-nothing framing, and similar shortcuts. They speed judgment and often protect mood or ego short-term — while quietly degrading decision quality.

This model is a checklist for self-audit: name the distortion, separate event from interpretation, then choose again with a cleaner map.

### Diagram

```text
  automatic thought → label distortion → evidence for/against → rewrite → decide
```

# When to use

- A decision feels urgent and emotionally loud beyond the stakes.
- You notice absolute language: always, never, disaster, perfect.
- Feedback or data keep bouncing off a fixed story.
- You want a pre-mortem on your own reasoning before committing.

Skip it when the conflict is belief vs behavior coherence (use [Cognitive Dissonance Theory](/models/understand-self/cognitive-dissonance.md)), or when the issue is deep unprovable axioms (use [Unthinkable Model](/models/understand-self/unthinkable-model.md)).

# How it works

### Common distortions (decision-relevant)

1. **All-or-nothing** — if it is not perfect, it is worthless.
2. **Overgeneralization** — one event becomes a universal law.
3. **Catastrophizing** — jump to the worst vivid ending.
4. **Mind-reading** — assume others’ motives without evidence.
5. **Fortune-telling** — treat a feared future as certain.
6. **Should-statements** — rigid rules that shame rather than guide.
7. **Emotional reasoning** — “I feel it, therefore it is true.”
8. **Personalization** — claim blame (or credit) beyond your control.
9. **Filtering** — notice only the negative (or only the flattering) data.
10. **Labeling** — replace a behavior with a total identity (“I am a failure”).

### Correction loop

1. **Catch** — write the hot thought exactly.
2. **Label** — which distortion(s)?
3. **Evidence** — for / against; what would a camera show?
4. **Alternative** — a balanced sentence you could defend to a skeptic.
5. **Decide** — act on the revised map, not the first flare.

### Practice tip

Keep a one-week log: situation → automatic thought → distortion name → revised thought. Patterns (your “favorites”) matter more than any single slip. Pair with [Potential Trap](/models/understand-self/potential-trap.md) when fortune-telling is the main mode.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Decision** — What decision or judgment feels loaded right now?
2. **Thought** — Write the automatic thought driving it.
3. **Label** — Which distortion fits (catastrophizing, mind-reading, all-or-nothing, etc.)?
4. **Evidence** — What facts support and contradict the thought?
5. **Rewrite** — Restate a more accurate thought; how does the decision change?
