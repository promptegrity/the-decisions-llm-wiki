# Overview

The Critique Model is a way to **receive** praise and criticism without collapsing into defensiveness or empty flattery-chasing. You separate the signal (what to change or keep) from the noise (tone, ego, politics), then decide what to do with it.

Hearing feedback well is a skill: compliments can hide vague encouragement; criticism can hide useful constraints — or someone else’s fear.

### Diagram

```text
  feedback in → filter (signal vs noise) → keep / change work → discard rest
```

# When to use

- You get mixed or emotionally charged reactions to your work.
- You tend to dismiss praise (“they’re just being nice”) or overreact to critique.
- A review conversation is coming and you want a listening structure.
- Feedback feels personal and you need a way to process it into action.

Skip it when you need a private learning loop against your own predictions (use [Feedback Analysis](/models/learn/feedback-analysis.md)), when the issue is deciding whether to continue a project (use [Stop Rule](/models/choose/stop-rule.md) / [BCG Matrix](/models/prioritize/bcg-matrix.md)), or when you must decide fast with little input (use [Yes/No Rule](/models/choose/yes-no-rule.md)).

# How it works

### Receiving compliments

1. **Pause** — do not deflect (“it was nothing”).
2. **Clarify** — “What specifically worked for you?” Vague praise teaches nothing.
3. **Store the pattern** — note what to **keep doing**, not only how good it felt.
4. **Thank** without negotiating the compliment away.

### Receiving criticism

1. **Listen fully** — no immediate rebuttal; take notes.
2. **Separate content from delivery** — harsh tone ≠ wrong content (and kind tone ≠ true content).
3. **Ask for examples** — one concrete instance beats abstract labels (“unprofessional,” “unclear”).
4. **Sort into three buckets**:
 - **Adopt** — change now
 - **Test** — try and verify
 - **Park** — not relevant / not actionable / wrong audience
5. **Reflect back** — “So you’re saying X; I’ll try Y.” Confirms understanding without surrendering judgment.

### Quick filter

| Question | If yes… |
|----------|---------|
| Is this about the work or about me as a person? | Work → learn; person-attack → bound it |
| Can I act on this within my control? | No → park or renegotiate scope |
| Would ignoring this repeat a real cost? | Yes → adopt or test |

### Practice tip

After any charged feedback, write three lines: **What they said**, **What I heard**, **What I’ll do (or not)**. That gap between said and heard is where most self-sabotage lives.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Input** — What compliment or criticism did you receive (exact words if possible)?
2. **Filter** — Useful signal vs noise/ego/agenda of the giver?
3. **Keep** — What will you keep or change in your work based on the useful part?
4. **Discard** — What will you explicitly not take on?
5. **Reply** — Optional: how will you respond without derailing yourself?
