# Overview

**Appreciative Inquiry** (often tied to David Cooperrider) is a stance and method: inquire into **what gives life** to a person, team, or system — strengths, peak moments, and shared aspirations — then design change from that energy. Contrasts with deficit talk that starts from problems and blame.

Core question: *what kind of interlocutor are you?* — one who mines failure, or one who amplifies what already works?

### Diagram

```text
  Discover (best of) → Dream → Design → Destiny/Deliver
```

# When to use

- Morale is low; problem-finding has become the culture’s only sport.
- You need buy-in for change and people shut down under critique.
- Interviews or coaching keep circling deficits and miss resources.
- You want generative questions that open possibility without denying hard facts.

Skip it when you must dig into governing assumptions behind repeated failure (use [Double-Loop Learning](/models/learn/double-loop-learning.md)) or when safety/failure analysis is the priority (use [Swiss Cheese](/models/uncertainty/swiss-cheese-model.md)).

# How it works

Classic **4-D** cycle (names vary slightly):

| Phase | Focus |
|-------|--------|
| **Discover** | Best of what is — stories of success, strengths, conditions that enabled them |
| **Dream** | What might be — vivid shared image of a desired future |
| **Design** | What should be — propositions, structures, and practices that embody the dream |
| **Destiny / Deliver** | How to sustain — experiments, commitments, learning loops |

### Appreciative questions (examples)

1. When were we at our best — and what made that possible?
2. What do we value most about working together?
3. Without constraints, what would we want more of?
4. What small step would move us toward that picture this week?

### How to act as interlocutor

1. **Listen for vitality** — energy, pride, skill — not only gaps.
2. **Reflect strengths back** so they become usable resources.
3. **Link past peaks to future design** — don’t leave appreciation as flattery.
4. **Still name constraints** — appreciation is not denial; it chooses the *entry point*.

### Practice tip

In your next difficult conversation, open with two genuine discovery questions before any diagnosis. If you cannot find anything worth amplifying, you may not yet understand the other side’s world.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Topic** — What relationship or team conversation are we reframing?
2. **Best of** — What already works or worked at its best here?
3. **Dream** — What desired future would we build from those strengths?
4. **Design** — What practices would make that future more likely?
5. **Destiny** — What is the first appreciative action or question you will use live?
