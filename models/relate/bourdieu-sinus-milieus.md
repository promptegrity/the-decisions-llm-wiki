# Overview

This page pairs **Pierre Bourdieu’s** view of social space (economic, cultural, and social capital; habitus; distinction) with **Sinus-Milieus** — research clusters of people who share values, lifestyle, and status. Together they answer: *where does this person or segment sit socially, and what feels “natural” to them?*

The point is not to stereotype — it is to stop treating preferences as purely individual when they are also **positional**.

### Diagram

```text
  Social space (sketch):
  high cultural capital
        ^
        |  milieu clusters (taste, lifestyle)
        +------------------> economic capital
```

# When to use

- Marketing, product, or political messaging fails because you assumed “everyone” shares your taste.
- You need segments richer than age/income alone.
- Conflict looks like values clash but is also about status and belonging.
- You wonder why the “rational” offer doesn’t land with a group that has different capital and habitus.

Skip it when the question is universal motive levels (use [Maslow](/models/relate/maslow-pyramid.md)) or how a product spreads across adopters (use [Diffusion Model](/models/systems/diffusion-model.md)).

# How it works

### Bourdieu — social space in brief

| Idea | Meaning for decisions |
|------|------------------------|
| **Economic capital** | Money, assets, financial security |
| **Cultural capital** | Education, tastes, credentials, “knowing the code” |
| **Social capital** | Networks, memberships, who will take your call |
| **Habitus** | Embodied dispositions — what feels obvious, vulgar, or aspirational |
| **Distinction** | Taste often signals and defends position |

People with similar volumes and compositions of capital tend to cluster; their choices look free but are patterned.

### Sinus-Milieus — lifestyle groups

Sinus maps populations into milieus combining **social status** (low → high) and **basic values** (tradition → modernization → reorientation). Exact milieu names vary by country and year; the decision use is:

1. Place the audience on **status × values**.
2. Ask what that milieu **aspires to**, **fears**, and **rejects**.
3. Adapt language, channels, and symbols — not only price and features.

### Questions to place a person or segment

1. What **capital** do they have — and which kind matters most in their field?
2. What would feel like **upgrading** vs **betraying** their milieu?
3. Are you selling to their **current** position or to an **aspirational** one?
4. Which signals (brand, venue, jargon) read as in-group vs outsider?

### How to act

1. **Map before message** — status and values before features.
2. **Match codes** — design, tone, and proof points to the milieu’s distinction game.
3. **Don’t flatten** — adjacent milieus may share income but reject each other’s aesthetics.
4. **Check your own habitus** — teams often design for themselves.

### Practice tip

For any campaign or change initiative, name the milieu you are *actually* speaking to and one you are *accidentally* alienating. If you can’t name either, you are still inside your own bubble.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Subject** — Who are we placing (person, segment, audience)?
2. **Capitals** — Roughly: economic, cultural, social — what do they have/value?
3. **Milieu** — Which lifestyle world (values, aesthetics, status games) fits best?
4. **Misread** — Where might you be projecting *your* milieu onto them?
5. **Adapt** — How will message, gift, or offer change given that place?
