# Overview

The Expectation Management Model warns that disappointment is often not caused by bad reality alone, but by **unexamined expectations**. In the sharp form: satisfaction ≈ **experience − expectation**. Looking for a “soulmate” (perfect partner, perfect hire, perfect team, perfect product) sets the bar so high that even strong outcomes feel like failure.

The practical move is not cynicism — it is to **surface, negotiate, and update** expectations before they harden into silent contracts.

### Diagram

```text
  Satisfaction ≈ Experience − Expectation
  Raise experience and/or reset expectation (out loud)
```

# When to use

- Hiring, dating metaphors for partnership, vendor selection, or “culture fit” debates.
- Stakeholders say “I thought you meant…” after delivery.
- Morale drops despite objectively decent results.
- You are about to overpromise to win a yes.

Skip it when the constraint physics are the real issue (use [Project Management Triangle](/models/prioritize/project-management-triangle.md)) or when you need to resolve an open conflict process (use [Conflict Resolution Model](/models/relate/conflict-resolution-model.md)).

# How it works

Treat every important relationship as having three layers:

| Layer | Content | Failure mode |
|-------|---------|--------------|
| **Said expectations** | Explicit promises, SLAs, job descriptions | Still incomplete if vague |
| **Assumed expectations** | “Obviously we’ll…” never spoken | The usual source of betrayal feelings |
| **Ideal expectations** | Soulmate fantasy / perfect outcome | Guarantees chronic shortfall |

### Questions

1. What does “good” look like **in observable terms** for both sides?
2. Which hopes are **needs**, which are **preferences**, which are **fantasies**?
3. What will we do when reality is **80%** of the ideal — celebrate, renegotiate, or exit?
4. Have we scheduled a moment to **revise** expectations after new information?

### How to act

1. **Write the unspoken** — convert assumptions into checkable statements.
2. **Under-promise with integrity**, then aim to meet or beat — not to sandbag forever.
3. **Separate person from role** — love the human; contract the collaboration.
4. **Update early** when facts change; late surprise is expectation debt with interest.

### Practice tip

Before kickoff (or a serious personal commitment), each side lists top five expectations. Swap lists. Anything not on both lists is a risk — discuss it now.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Relationship** — Whose expectations are we managing (partner, client, team, self)?
2. **Formula** — Satisfaction ≈ experience − expectation: where is the gap now?
3. **Promised** — What was implied or promised (even silently)?
4. **Reset** — What expectation must be renegotiated out loud?
5. **Deliver** — What experience will you make visible so the math works?
