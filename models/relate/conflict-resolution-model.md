# Overview

A **conflict resolution** lens separates **positions** (what people demand) from **interests** (why they want it), then seeks options that meet underlying interests without requiring total victory. “Elegance” means reducing heat, saving face, and finding a workable path — not winning the argument.

Useful when others’ goals collide with yours or with each other.

### Diagram

```text
  Positions (demands)
       |
       v
  Interests (needs/fears) → options → small agreement
```

# When to use

- Disagreement is stuck on incompatible demands.
- Escalation (blame, coalitions, silence) is starting to cost more than the issue.
- You need a structured conversation, not another round of advocacy.
- Trust is damaged but cooperation still has value (see also [Prisoner's Dilemma](/models/relate/prisoners-dilemma.md)).

Skip it when the issue is systemic failure layers (use [Swiss Cheese](/models/uncertainty/swiss-cheese-model.md)) or when you mainly need an appreciative reopen of dialogue (use [Appreciative Inquiry](/models/relate/appreciative-inquiry.md)).

# How it works

### Core moves

| Step | Do this |
|------|---------|
| 1. **Cool the frame** | Separate people from the problem; lower threat to identity |
| 2. **Map positions** | What does each side say they must have? |
| 3. **Uncover interests** | Needs, fears, constraints behind the positions |
| 4. **Invent options** | Multiple packages before committing to one |
| 5. **Use criteria** | Fair standards (precedent, market, principle) over power plays |
| 6. **Agree path** | Clear next steps, owners, and how to reopen if it fails |

### Escalation awareness

Conflicts often climb: disagreement → personalization → coalition → deadlock. Intervene earlier with interest talk; later stages need more face-saving and structure.

### Questions

1. What does each side **need** that the current demand is a proxy for?
2. What would a **good enough** outcome look like for both (not perfect)?
3. Where can we create **value** (trade across issues) instead of splitting one pie?
4. What **process** feels fair even if the content is hard?

### How to act

1. **Listen to restate interests** until the other side feels heard.
2. **Offer choices**, not ultimatums — elegance needs dignity.
3. **Trade** across differently valued issues (time vs scope vs recognition).
4. **Write agreements** simply; ambiguity restarts the fight.

### Practice tip

In the next stuck conflict, ban debating positions for ten minutes. Each person may only state interests and constraints. Options usually appear only after that constraint.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Parties** — Who is in the conflict, and what is each *position* (stated demand)?
2. **Interests** — Under each position, what need or fear is at stake?
3. **Escalation** — Where are you on the escalation ladder (debate vs attack vs exit)?
4. **Options** — Invent options that serve interests without demanding full surrender.
5. **Agreement** — What small, checkable next step can both accept?
