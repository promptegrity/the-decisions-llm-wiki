# Overview

Maslow’s Pyramid (hierarchy of needs) orders motives from **survival and safety** up through **belonging and esteem** to **self-actualization**. The claim: lower, unmet needs tend to dominate attention; higher aspirations become salient when basics feel secure.

Use it as a lens on **what drives someone** (customer, colleague, partner) — not as a rigid law of personality.

### Diagram

```text
              /\
             /  \     Self-actualization
            /----\
           /      \   Esteem
          /--------\
         /          \ Belonging
        /------------\
       /              \ Safety
      /----------------\
     /                  \ Physiological
    /____________________\
```

# When to use

- You are designing a product, offer, or message and must guess which need it actually serves.
- Conflict or “irrational” behavior looks clearer once you see which need is threatened.
- Motivation talks stay stuck on “inspiration” while people lack security or belonging.
- You confuse your wants (status, novelty) with others’ unmet needs (safety, recognition).

Skip it when the question is social class and taste milieus (use [Bourdieu / Sinus-Milieus](/models/relate/bourdieu-sinus-milieus.md)) or when the issue is how innovations spread (use [Diffusion Model](/models/systems/diffusion-model.md)).

# How it works

Classic five-level stack (bottom → top):

| Level | Need | Rough question |
|-------|------|----------------|
| 1 | **Physiological** | Food, sleep, health, basic comfort? |
| 2 | **Safety** | Stability, job, shelter, predictability? |
| 3 | **Belonging / love** | Acceptance, relationships, group membership? |
| 4 | **Esteem** | Respect, competence, status, achievement? |
| 5 | **Self-actualization** | Growth, meaning, creative potential? |

(Later variants add cognitive, aesthetic, and transcendence levels; the decision use is the same: **which level is active now?**)

### Questions to place a person or offer

1. **What is threatened or missing?** Anxiety often points to a lower level.
2. **What are they optimizing for in language?** “Secure,” “fit in,” “be seen,” “become”?
3. **Would my pitch land if their safety or belonging is shaky?** Higher-level messages often bounce.

### How to act

1. **Diagnose before prescribe** — don’t sell self-actualization to someone fighting for safety.
2. **Match the intervention** to the active level (pay, process, community, recognition, growth path).
3. **Expect mixed motives** — people chase several levels at once; the pyramid is a priority heuristic, not a queue they finish forever.
4. **Check your projection** — your top of the pyramid may be their middle.

### Practice tip

In a stakeholder interview, ask what would make them feel *safe*, *included*, and *respected* before asking about vision. Needs lower on the stack often explain resistance that looks like “no ambition.”

### Facilitation steps

1. **Person/offer** — Who or what situation are we reading (self, other, customer)?
2. **Level** — Which need level seems most active or blocked right now?
3. **Mismatch** — Are you offering/asking at a level that doesn’t match?
4. **Respect lower** — What lower need must be safe before higher ones land?
5. **Move** — One action that addresses the real level (not the aspirational pitch)?
