# Overview

The Gift Dilemma is a decision aid for **what to give and to whom**. A gift is never only an object: it signals how you see the relationship, how much attention you paid, and what you expect in return (or refuse to expect). Choosing badly is rarely about price — it is about mismatch.

Use the model to separate *thoughtfulness* from *expense*, and *obligation* from *affection*.

### Diagram

```text
                 High closeness      Low closeness
  High stakes  | personal & specific | safe & respectful
  Low stakes   | small & knowing     | light / optional
```

# When to use

- Birthdays, holidays, host gifts, thank-yous, or “I should bring something.”
- You feel stuck between generic, extravagant, and “maybe nothing.”
- The relationship is uneven (boss, new acquaintance, close friend, family) and norms are unclear.
- You want the gesture to land without creating awkward debt.

Skip it when the decision is a large purchase for yourself (use [Purchase Decision Model](/models/choose/purchase-decision-model.md)) or when the real issue is a deeper dilemma about the relationship (use [Rubber Band Model](/models/choose/rubber-band-model.md)).

# How it works

Sort candidates along two axes that matter more than catalog browsing:

1. **Closeness / knowledge of the person** — How well do you know their tastes, needs, and boundaries?
2. **Symbolic weight of the occasion** — Is this a light courtesy or a high-stakes ritual?

### Placement guide

| | **High closeness** | **Low closeness** |
|--|--------------------|-------------------|
| **High-stakes occasion** | **Personal & specific** — something only they would notice; effort over brand | **Safe & respectful** — quality classic, consumable, or shared experience; avoid intimacy you have not earned |
| **Low-stakes occasion** | **Small & knowing** — inside joke, useful trifle, time together | **Light & optional** — modest token or a sincere note; do not over-invest |

### Questions before you buy

1. **Is this for them — or for how I want to look?** Status gifts often serve the giver.
2. **Will this create obligation?** Expensive gifts can pressure people who cannot reciprocate.
3. **Is experience better than object?** Shared time often outperforms stuff when closeness is high.
4. **Would nothing (or a letter) be cleaner?** Sometimes presence and words beat a forced object.

### Practice tip

Decide the *category* first (personal / safe / small / light), then shop inside that box. The Gift Dilemma is a framing tool — it reduces wrong gifts more than it invents perfect ones.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Occasion** — Who is the gift for, and how high-stakes is the occasion?
2. **Closeness** — How well do you know their tastes and boundaries?
3. **Place** — Using the closeness × stakes guide: which box are you in?
4. **Candidates** — List 2–3 gift ideas that fit that box (effort vs intimacy).
5. **Choose** — Pick one; what signal should it send, and what debt should it avoid?
