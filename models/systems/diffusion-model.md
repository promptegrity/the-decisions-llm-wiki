# Overview

The **Diffusion of Innovations** model (Everett Rogers) explains how new ideas and products spread through a social system: from **innovators** and **early adopters** through the **early and late majority** to **laggards**. Hits look inevitable afterward; during diffusion they depend on social proof, channels, and whether the idea crosses the chasm between enthusiasts and the mainstream.

### Diagram

```text
  Adopters over time (sketch):

  innovators  early    early     late      laggards
     |        adopters majority  majority     |
     |          |         |         |         |
     *----------*---------*---------*---------*
              (S-curve of cumulative adoption)
```

# When to use

- You wonder why a “better” product stalls while a peer becomes the default.
- Go-to-market assumes all customers adopt for the same reasons.
- You need to time messaging, pricing, or features for the current adopter wave.
- Network effects or status among peers clearly matter.

Skip it when the economics of niches vs hits is the core question (use [Long Tail](/models/systems/long-tail-model.md)) or when social position/taste milieus need mapping first (use [Bourdieu / Sinus](/models/relate/bourdieu-sinus-milieus.md)).

# How it works

### Adopter categories (classic bell curve over time)

| Group | Share (approx.) | Motive sketch |
|-------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Innovators** | ~2.5% | Novelty, tech curiosity, risk tolerance |
| **Early adopters** | ~13.5% | Vision, status as opinion leaders |
| **Early majority** | ~34% | Practicality, proof, peer references |
| **Late majority** | ~34% | Skepticism, pressure, lower risk |
| **Laggards** | ~16% | Tradition, necessity only |

### What makes an innovation spread (Rogers’ attributes)

1. **Relative advantage** — better than the status quo in the adopter’s eyes.
2. **Compatibility** — fits values and practices.
3. **Complexity** — easier wins.
4. **Trialability** — can try small.
5. **Observability** — results are visible to others.

### Questions

1. Which **adopter group** are we selling to *now* — and with whose language?
2. Do we have **social proof** that the next group trusts?
3. Is the product stuck with innovators (cool but not usable/credible for the majority)?
4. Which attribute (complexity, trial, visibility) is the bottleneck?

### How to act

1. **Sequence the offer** — enthusiasts ≠ mainstream; change proof and packaging as you move right on the curve.
2. **Recruit early adopters** as bridges — their endorsement moves the early majority.
3. **Reduce friction** for the majority: simplify, guarantee, demonstrate.
4. **Don’t declare failure too early** — or success too early — based only on innovator love.

### Practice tip

Plot current customers on the adopter curve. If 90% of fans are innovators and your roadmap still optimizes for novelty, you are not building the bridge the early majority needs.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Innovation** — What idea/product is spreading (or failing to)?
2. **Stage** — Which adopter group are you in or targeting (innovators→laggards)?
3. **Attributes** — Advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, observability — weakest link?
4. **Blocker** — Why might the chasm between early and majority exist here?
5. **Move** — One action to improve diffusion for the next adopter group?
