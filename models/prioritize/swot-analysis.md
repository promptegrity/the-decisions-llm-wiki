# Overview

SWOT Analysis maps a choice or situation into four boxes: **Strengths**, **Weaknesses**, **Opportunities**, and **Threats**. The goal is not a pretty grid — it is to see the real trade-offs before you pick a solution, so you build on what works, fix what does not, and avoid surprises from outside.

Internal factors (strengths / weaknesses) are under your control; external factors (opportunities / threats) are in the environment. Pairing them shows where to act, where to wait, and where a “good idea” is actually fragile.

### Diagram

```text
              Helpful              Harmful
           +------------------+------------------+
  Internal | STRENGTHS        | WEAKNESSES       |
           +------------------+------------------+
  External | OPPORTUNITIES    | THREATS          |
           +------------------+------------------+
```

# When to use

- You face several plausible options and need a structured comparison.
- You are about to commit resources (time, money, reputation) and want a reality check.
- A plan feels exciting but you have not stress-tested risks or blind spots.
- You need a shared language with others to debate a decision without arguing from vibes.

Skip it when you only need a fast binary call (use [Yes/No Rule](/models/choose/yes-no-rule.md)), when the problem is too many options rather than unclear ones (use [Jam Paradox](/models/choose/jam-paradox.md) to prune first), or when you already know the goal is wrong (use [SMART Model](/models/prioritize/smart-model.md)).

# How it works

Draw a 2×2 (or four lists):

| | **Helpful** | **Harmful** |
|--|-------------|-------------|
| **Internal** | **Strengths** — skills, assets, advantages you already have | **Weaknesses** — gaps, constraints, habits that hurt this choice |
| **External** | **Opportunities** — openings, trends, timing you can use | **Threats** — risks, competition, constraints outside you |

### Questions for each box

1. **Strengths** — What do I already do well that this option leans on? What would others say I bring?
2. **Weaknesses** — What am I missing, delaying, or underestimating? Where have similar efforts failed for me?
3. **Opportunities** — What external conditions favor this path now? What door closes if I wait?
4. **Threats** — What could kill or dilute the upside? Who or what could block me?

### How to turn SWOT into a decision

1. Prefer options that **use strengths** and **ride opportunities**.
2. Treat big **weaknesses × threats** combinations as deal-breakers or redesign prompts — not footnotes.
3. Ask: can I **convert** a weakness into a manageable cost, or neutralize a threat with a strength?
4. Compare 2–3 candidate solutions on the same SWOT grid; pick the one with the best *usable* upside and the most honest downside plan.

### Practice tip

Write bullets that are **specific and falsifiable** (“I already have three clients in this niche”) rather than slogans (“I’m creative”). Vague SWOT produces confident wrong answers.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Decision** — What decision or option are we stress-testing with SWOT?
2. **Internal** — List Strengths and Weaknesses that matter for this decision (facts you own).
3. **External** — List Opportunities and Threats in the environment (outside your direct control).
4. **Cross** — Which strength unlocks which opportunity? Which weakness meets which threat?
5. **Decide** — Given the filled SWOT, what do you do: pursue, reshape, or drop?
