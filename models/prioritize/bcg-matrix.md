# Overview

The BCG Matrix (Boston Consulting Group) places initiatives on two axes: **relative payoff / growth potential** versus **resource cost / market share of effort**. In personal use it answers: where should scarce time and money go — and what should you milk, feed, or kill?

Originally built for product portfolios (Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks, Dogs). Applied to yourself, each “product” is a project, skill, role, or habit competing for the same budget of attention.

### Diagram

```text
  Payoff / growth potential
        high |  INVEST/FEED     QUESTION
             |  (star-like)     (maybe)
             |
        low  |  HARVEST/MILK    DROP
             |  (cash cow-like) (dog-like)
             +----------------------------> resource cost
                 low              high
```

# When to use

- You juggle several initiatives and feel everything is “important.”
- You need to decide what to fund, maintain, or cut based on return, not guilt.
- You are reallocating after a busy season: what still earns its keep?
- Cost is rising on something that no longer grows.

Skip it when you need a day-to-day task sort (use [Eisenhower Matrix](/models/prioritize/eisenhower-matrix.md)), when the goal itself is unclear (use [SMART Model](/models/prioritize/smart-model.md)), or when you only need a map of projects without investment logic (use [Project Portfolio](/models/prioritize/project-portfolio.md)).

# How it works

Plot each initiative on a 2×2 (adapt labels to personal work):

| | **High payoff / growth** | **Low payoff / growth** |
|--|--------------------------|-------------------------|
| **Low cost / easy to sustain** | **Stars** — high return, manageable cost; protect and grow | **Cash cows** — steady return; maintain with minimal drama |
| **High cost / hard to sustain** | **Question marks** — promising but expensive; decide: invest or exit | **Dogs** — low return, high drain; cut or radically redesign |

Classic corporate labels: Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks, Dogs — same logic, different nouns.

### Questions to place each item

1. **Payoff** — If this works, how much does it move money, skill, reputation, or well-being?
2. **Cost** — What does it take weekly (hours, stress, money, opportunity cost)?
3. **Trend** — Is payoff rising, flat, or fading? Is cost creeping up?

### How to act by quadrant

1. **Stars** — give them priority capacity; do not starve them for Dogs.
2. **Cash cows** — keep efficient; avoid over-investing in polish that does not raise return.
3. **Question marks** — set a time-boxed bet with a kill criterion; do not let them linger as vague hopes.
4. **Dogs** — stop, sell, or demote; sunk cost is not a reason to continue.

### Practice tip

Force every item into one box **before** debating feelings. Then check: are your Stars actually getting your best hours, or are Dogs eating the calendar?

### Facilitation steps

1. **Inventory** — List the efforts/projects competing for your scarce time or money.
2. **Axes** — For each: relative payoff/potential vs resource cost (high/low is enough).
3. **Place** — Assign each to a box (e.g. invest / maintain / harvest / drop — using your BCG labels).
4. **Imbalance** — Where is energy stuck on low-payoff / high-cost items?
5. **Shift** — What one reallocation will you make this month?
