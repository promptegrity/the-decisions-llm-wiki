# Overview

The **Pareto Principle** (80/20 rule) observes that in many systems a **minority of inputs** drives a **majority of outputs**: ~20% of customers, bugs, features, or causes often account for ~80% of revenue, complaints, value, or effects. Named after Vilfredo Pareto’s observation on wealth distribution; popularized as a management heuristic by Joseph Juran and others.

The point is not the exact ratio — it is **unevenness**: find the vital few.

### Diagram

```text
  Impact
    |**
    | ***

      vital few     trivial many
```

# When to use

- Too many initiatives; you need focus without pretending all items are equal.
- Quality or support load is dominated by a few recurring issues.
- Sales, content, or usage data look “flat” until you sort by impact.
- You are allocating scarce time, budget, or attention across many options.

Skip it when the strategic edge is the *many small niches* (use [Long Tail](/models/systems/long-tail-model.md)) or when you need urgency×importance sorting of tasks (use [Eisenhower](/models/prioritize/eisenhower-matrix.md)).

# How it works

1. **Pick an outcome** worth caring about (revenue, defects, time burned, influence).
2. **List contributing factors** (products, clients, causes, activities).
3. **Rank by contribution** — measure if you can; estimate if you must.
4. **Look for a steep head** — a small set that explains most of the outcome.
5. **Act on the vital few**; consciously deprioritize or systematize the trivial many.

### Typical 80/20 patterns

| Domain | Vital few often are… |
|--------|----------------------|
| Sales | A small share of customers / SKUs |
| Bugs | A few root causes or modules |
| Work | A few activities that move the goal |
| Complaints | A few friction points in the journey |

### Questions

1. If we could only keep 20% of this list, which items preserve most of the value?
2. Which 20% of problems create 80% of the pain?
3. Are we spreading effort evenly out of fairness — or out of fear of choosing?

### How to act

1. **Measure once** — even a rough rank beats equal treatment of unequals.
2. **Invest disproportionately** in the head of the distribution.
3. **Don’t ignore the tail blindly** — some tail items are strategic options or risk seeds (see Long Tail / Black Swan).
4. **Recompute** — yesterday’s vital few drift.

### Practice tip

Before a planning meeting, force a one-page ranked list of drivers of the main KPI. Debate only the top slice until it is owned; park the rest.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Outcome** — Which result do you care about (revenue, time burned, defects, impact)?
2. **Factors** — List contributing activities, clients, causes, or items.
3. **Rank** — Order them by contribution (measure if you can; estimate if you must).
4. **Vital few** — Which small set drives most of the outcome?
5. **Act** — What will you double down on, and what will you consciously deprioritize?
