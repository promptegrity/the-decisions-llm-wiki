# Overview

The Project Management Triangle (also called the iron triangle) states a hard limit: for a given quality bar, you cannot freely optimize **scope**, **time**, and **cost** together. Push one corner and at least one other must give. “Perfect” — everything, now, cheap, flawless — is not a plan; it is a refusal to choose.

Use the triangle to make trade-offs **explicit** with stakeholders instead of discovering them as missed deadlines and quiet quality cuts.

### Diagram

```text
            SCOPE
           /      \
          /        \
         /          \
     TIME ---------- COST
   (You can optimize two — the third gives.)
```

# When to use

- Stakeholders demand more features, sooner, with the same budget.
- A project is “red” and people argue about blame instead of constraints.
- You must negotiate a change request or a recovery plan.
- Quality is slipping because nobody admitted which corner moved.

Skip it when the question is personal task priority (use [Eisenhower Matrix](/models/prioritize/eisenhower-matrix.md)) or when you need a portfolio view across many projects (use [Project Portfolio](/models/prioritize/project-portfolio.md)).

# How it works

Draw three corners; quality (or “good enough”) sits in the middle as the outcome of the three.

| Corner | Means | If you tighten it… |
|--------|-------|--------------------|
| **Scope** | What must be delivered | …you need more time and/or cost, or quality falls |
| **Time** | Deadline / duration | …you cut scope and/or raise cost (people, overtime, risk) |
| **Cost** | Budget / resources | …you cut scope and/or extend time, or accept quality risk |

Classic shorthand: **good, fast, cheap — pick two.**

### Questions for every change request

1. Which corner is **fixed** (non-negotiable)?
2. Which corner is **flexible**?
3. What happens to **quality** if we pretend all three stay fixed?
4. Who owns the trade-off decision — and has that person agreed in writing?

### How to act

1. **Name the fixed corner first** (often time or budget in real organizations).
2. **Propose two options**, each sacrificing a different flexible corner — force a choice.
3. **Re-baseline** when a corner moves; do not keep the old plan as fiction.
4. **Protect quality explicitly** — if quality is sacred, treat it as a fourth constraint and shrink scope earlier.

### Practice tip

In status meetings, ban “we’ll just try harder” as a plan. Require a sentence of the form: “We keep X fixed; we change Y; Z absorbs the impact.”

### Facilitation steps

1. **Project** — Name the project and the current ask (scope/time/cost pressure).
2. **Triangle** — Which side is fixed right now: scope, time, or cost/resources?
3. **Trade-off** — If one side is fixed, which other side will give?
4. **Say no** — What “perfect” expectation must you drop out loud?
5. **Agree** — What is the negotiated triangle for the next milestone?
