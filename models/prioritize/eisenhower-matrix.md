# Overview

The Eisenhower Matrix sorts what competes for your attention into four boxes: **urgent vs not urgent**, crossed with **important vs not important**. The point is not to do more — it is to stop treating every ping as a priority and to protect work that matters but does not shout.

Named after Dwight D. Eisenhower’s distinction between what is important and what is merely urgent.

### Diagram

```text
                 URGENT          NOT URGENT
              +---------------+---------------+
   IMPORTANT  | 1. DO         | 2. SCHEDULE   |
              +---------------+---------------+
NOT IMPORTANT | 3. DELEGATE   | 4. ELIMINATE  |
              +---------------+---------------+
```

# When to use

- Your to-do list is long, reactive, or always “on fire.”
- You finish many tasks but still feel behind on what actually matters.
- You need a weekly (or daily) reset: what to do now, schedule, delegate, or drop.
- You are choosing between depth work and interruptions.

Skip it when the real problem is *which goal* to pursue (use [SMART](/models/prioritize/smart-model.md) first) or when you need a portfolio view across many projects (use [Project Portfolio](/models/prioritize/project-portfolio.md)).

# How it works

Draw (or imagine) a 2×2:

| | **Urgent** | **Not urgent** |
|--|------------|----------------|
| **Important** | **1. Do** — crises, deadlines, problems that matter now | **2. Schedule** — planning, relationships, learning, deep work |
| **Not important** | **3. Delegate** — interruptions, some meetings, other people’s urgencies | **4. Eliminate** — distractions, busywork, habit noise |

### Questions to place each item

1. **Important?** Does this move a real goal, value, or commitment — or only reduce anxiety / please someone else’s timeline?
2. **Urgent?** Does delay create real damage soon — or only discomfort?

### How to act by quadrant

1. **Do (important + urgent)** — handle personally and soon; keep this box small by investing in quadrant 2.
2. **Schedule (important + not urgent)** — block time; this is where effectiveness compounds.
3. **Delegate (not important + urgent)** — pass on, automate, or set a lighter response rule.
4. **Eliminate (not important + not urgent)** — cut or ignore without guilt.

### Practice tip

Re-sort the list, then **time-box quadrant 2** before the day fills with quadrant 1 and 3. Efficiency here means fewer false urgencies, not faster busywork.

### Facilitation steps

1. **List** — What tasks or commitments are competing for your attention right now? Name 5–15 concrete items (not vague themes).
2. **Important?** — For each item: does this move a real goal or value, or only reduce anxiety / please someone else’s timeline?
3. **Urgent?** — For each item: does delay create real damage soon, or only discomfort?
4. **Place** — Put each item in one box: Do / Schedule / Delegate / Eliminate.
5. **Act** — What is the single next action for Do, and when will you time-box Schedule (quadrant 2) this week?
