# Overview

The Team Model asks a blunt question: is this group actually a *team worth investing in*, or just people sharing a calendar? A useful team needs three things at once — **skills** (can they do the work?), **motivation** (will they?), and **chemistry** (can they work *together*?). Miss any one and output collapses even if résumés look strong.

The point is not to romanticize teamwork. It is to diagnose which factor is weak before you add headcount, pressure, or process.

### Diagram

```text
  Team worth checking:
     Skills  ∩  Motivation  ∩  Chemistry
            (all three needed)
```

# When to use

- You are forming, hiring into, or restructuring a team.
- Output is poor despite “good people” on paper.
- Conflict, silence, or freeloading keep recurring.
- You must decide: invest, reshuffle, or disband.

Skip it when the real problem is unclear goals (use [SMART](/models/prioritize/smart-model.md)) or when you need a stage-by-stage development path (use [Team Performance Model](/models/collaborate/team-performance-model.md)).

# How it works

Score the team on three dimensions. All three must be “good enough”; excellence in one does not rescue a zero in another.

| Dimension | Question | Weak signal |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| **Skills** | Do we have the competences this outcome requires? | Rework, dependency on one hero, chronic “we don’t know how” |
| **Motivation** | Do people care enough to push through friction? | Missed commitments, minimal effort, energy only when watched |
| **Chemistry** | Can we coordinate, trust, and disagree productively? | Politics, silence, duplicate work, blame loops |

### Diagnostic questions

1. **Skills** — If we swapped this team for another with the same motivation and chemistry, would capability still be the bottleneck?
2. **Motivation** — Is the work meaningful, fair, and owned — or only assigned?
3. **Chemistry** — Can people say hard things early, or do problems surface only after damage?

### How to act

1. **Skills gap** — train, hire, pair, or shrink scope; do not “motivate” your way out of missing competence.
2. **Motivation gap** — clarify purpose, stakes, recognition, and autonomy; remove blockers and unfair load.
3. **Chemistry gap** — reset norms, roles, and feedback; sometimes the fix is a people mix change, not a workshop.
4. **All three weak** — stop adding process; redesign the team or the mission.

### Practice tip

Rate each dimension 1–5 with the team (or privately first). The lowest score is usually the lever — not the loudest complaint.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Team** — Name the group and its purpose in one line.
2. **Skills** — Do we have the skills for the job? Gaps?
3. **Motivation** — Is energy aligned with the purpose?
4. **Chemistry** — Can people work together under stress?
5. **Verdict** — Worth investing, reshaping, or not a real team yet — and first fix?
