# Overview

The Roles Model shifts attention from **titles** (“engineer,” “PM,” “designer”) to **functions a healthy team needs** — for example: who challenges ideas, who finishes, who connects people, who researches, who keeps the goal alive. People often over-index on one preferred role; gaps and clashes show up as “personality problems” when they are really **role coverage** problems.

Changing your point of view means asking: *which role is missing, overplayed, or locked to the wrong person?* — not only *who is difficult*.

### Diagram


# When to use

- The team is talented but stuck, slow, or one-sided in discussion.
- The same person always critiques, always finishes, or always stays silent.
- You are staffing a project and want balance, not clones.
- Conflict repeats around “how we work,” not around facts.

Skip it when you first need to know if the team is even worth keeping (use [Team Model](/models/collaborate/team-model.md)), or when the constraint is scope/time/cost (use [Project Management Triangle](/models/prioritize/project-management-triangle.md)).

# How it works

Name the roles your work requires. A practical starter set (adapt names to your context):

| Role type | Contribution | Risk if missing / overdone |
|-----------|--------------|----------------------------|
| **Driver / shaper** | Pushes pace and priority | Missing → drift; overdone → steamrolling |
| **Challenger / critic** | Stress-tests ideas | Missing → groupthink; overdone → paralysis |
| **Finisher** | Closes details and quality | Missing → almost-done forever |
| **Coordinator** | Aligns people and decisions | Missing → parallel chaos |
| **Specialist** | Deep expertise | Missing → shallow work; overdone → silos |
| **Connector / team player** | Trust, glue, inclusion | Missing → brittle collaboration |
| **Explorer / researcher** | Options and outside info | Missing → tunnel vision |

### Questions to reframe the situation

1. **Coverage** — Which critical role has no owner this week?
2. **Load** — Who is stuck in one role and never allowed another?
3. **Clash** — Are two people fighting because both claim the same role?
4. **Perspective shift** — If I temporarily took the “finisher” or “challenger” lens, what would I notice?

### How to act

1. **Map people → roles** for the current project (one person can hold more than one).
2. **Fill gaps** by assignment, pairing, or hiring — not by hoping culture fixes it.
3. **Rotate lightly** so people stretch and the team does not depend on one hero role.
4. **Separate role conflict from personal conflict** in feedback conversations.

### Practice tip

In the next meeting, assign an explicit “challenger” and “finisher.” Changing who wears the hat often changes the outcome more than changing the agenda.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Situation** — What team outcome is struggling?
2. **Roles present** — Which complementary roles are filled (beyond job titles)?
3. **Missing** — Which role is absent or overloaded?
4. **Clash** — Where do two roles fight (e.g. challenger vs harmonizer)?
5. **Rebalance** — Who will take or drop a role temporarily, and how will you mark it?
