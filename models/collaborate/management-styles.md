# Overview

Management Styles treats leadership as a **choice of mode**, not a fixed personality. The classic spectrum runs from **directive** (you decide and instruct) to **participative** (the group decides with you) — with consultative and coaching styles in between. “Correct” leadership means fitting the style to **task urgency**, **competence**, and **willingness** of the people involved.

One style used always becomes a bottleneck: either you smother capable people, or you abandon people who still need structure.

### Diagram

```text
  Need for structure high → more directive
  Ownership capacity high → more participative
  (match style to moment, not personality alone)
```

# When to use

- You are taking over a team or changing how decisions get made.
- The same approach works for some people and fails for others.
- Crises, onboarding, and creative work are colliding under one default style.
- You need a shared language for “why did you decide alone / why did you open it up?”

Skip it when the issue is whether the *team itself* is viable (use [Team Model](/models/collaborate/team-model.md)) or when conflict between people is the core problem (use [Conflict Resolution Model](/models/relate/conflict-resolution-model.md)).

# How it works

Map the moment on two axes: **how much structure people need** and **how much ownership they can take**.

| Style | Leader behavior | Best when |
|-------|-----------------|-----------|
| **Directive** | Sets goals, methods, and deadlines; little discussion | Crisis, novices, safety-critical work, ambiguity that must end now |
| **Consultative** | Asks input, then decides | You need information and buy-in, but accountability stays with you |
| **Participative** | Co-decides with the group | Competent, motivated people; complex trade-offs; ownership matters |
| **Delegative** | Frames outcome, leaves method | High skill + high trust; you must free yourself for other work |

### Questions before you pick a style

1. **Competence** — Do they know how to do this, or only that it must be done?
2. **Motivation** — Will they push without close oversight?
3. **Stakes / time** — Is there room for learning and debate, or only for execution?
4. **You** — Are you clinging to control out of habit, or releasing control to avoid responsibility?

### How to act

1. **Match style to person × task**, not to your comfort zone.
2. **Explain the mode** (“I’m deciding this one; next sprint we decide together”) so shifts do not feel arbitrary.
3. **Move toward more autonomy** as skill and trust grow — that is how capacity compounds.
4. **Tighten again** under crisis or quality failure, then loosen when stability returns.

### Practice tip

For one week, label each significant decision with the style you used. If every label is the same, you are not leading situationally — you are repeating a habit.

### Facilitation steps

1. **Moment** — What decision or period of leadership is this about?
2. **Need** — How much structure do people need right now?
3. **Ownership** — How much ownership can they take?
4. **Style** — Choose more directive vs participative (or a mix) and why.
5. **Signal** — How will you communicate the style shift so it doesn’t look like mood?
